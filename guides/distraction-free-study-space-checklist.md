# Parent's Checklist: Creating a Distraction-Free Study Space at Home

An optimized study space at home helps children shift into "focus mode" easily. Use this checklist to audit and set up your child's home study environment.

---

## 📋 The 5-Step Study Space Checklist

### 1. Physical Location & Setup
- [ ] **Fixed Location:** The study spot is consistent daily (avoid beds, couches, or dynamic high-traffic areas).
- [ ] **Clutter Control:** Only books, stationery, and notebooks required for the current study session are on the desk.
- [ ] **Storage:** Extraneous papers, old assignments, and games are organized in nearby drawers or boxes out of direct sight.
- [ ] **Hydration & Nutrition:** A water bottle and light, healthy snacks are placed on the desk to minimize frequent trips to the kitchen.

### 2. Digital & Screen Time Management
- [ ] **Device Lockdown:** Non-essential smartphones, tablets, and gaming consoles are turned off or kept in another room.
- [ ] **Notification Block:** If a computer or tablet is required for active online study, notifications for games and messaging apps are disabled.
- [ ] **App Blocker (Optional):** Website blockers are active to prevent wandering off to social media or video streaming sites.
- *For detailed tips on managing screens, see our resource:* [Screen Time Guidelines for Children](https://doonvalleylucknow.com/pages/screen-time-guidelines-for-children-a-complete-guide-for-parents)

### 3. Lighting & Ergonomics
- [ ] **Positioning:** The study desk is placed to utilize natural light (preferably perpendicular to a window) to reduce eye strain.
- [ ] **Task Lighting:** A dedicated desk lamp (bright white light) is positioned to illuminate the study material without casting direct shadows.
- [ ] **Seating & Table:** The table and chair are at the correct height relative to the child's height, supporting upright posture (feet flat on the floor, elbows resting comfortably).

### 4. Noise & Household Routine
- [ ] **Quiet Hours:** Family members agree on designated "study hours" where TV volume is minimized and loud household conversations are limited.
- [ ] **Visual Boundary:** A clear boundary (e.g., closed door, simple partition) is maintained to limit visual interruptions from siblings or guests.
- [ ] **Focus Audios (Optional):** If ambient household noise is unavoidable, white noise or instrumental background music is used.

### 5. Scheduling & Child Involvement
- [ ] **Collaborative Design:** The child was involved in choosing desktop organizers or basic decorations to foster ownership of the space.
- [ ] **Structured Sessions:** Study time is scheduled in focused intervals (e.g., 30–45 minutes) followed by a strict 5-10 minute break.
- [ ] **Daily Clean-up:** The child has a routine of tidying up the desk at the end of each day's study session.
